# One annihilator at every tensor width

This scoped written proof was produced by Nick Petalas. The finite verifier
corroborates the new algebraic formulas. It is not a proof-kernel certificate.

## Statement and inherited operator

Use the exact historical eight-by-112 integer query matrix R retained by
Ali Romman's PR4958 and PR4720. Its columns are r_q, indexed by distinct
odd nonzero q in F_2^9. For every positive integer f the doubled Gram is

    A_f = (1/4) sum_q r_q r_q^T tensor Gamma_q^tensor(f).

The root coefficient occurs once. Gamma_q is the doubled action of
H_q = (X_q+Z_q)/sqrt(2). The Bell similarity and its exact signs are the
credited premises in PR4958, PROOF.md section 1. In Bell coordinates,
write c=a+b. Then

    Gamma_q |c,a> = (-1)^[(1+q.c)(q.a)] |c,a+(q.c)q>.

Dots and the exponent here are in F_2. In particular this action is a signed
permutation, and it fixes |0,0>. The verifier authenticates the actual query
and binds all its generators to the construction below. It does not rerun
the predecessor's Bell embedding or spectral certificates.

**Theorem.** There is one monic polynomial P in Q[x], independent of f,
with degree at most 2^93, such that P(A_f)=0 for every integer f>=1.
Consequently the scalar minimal-polynomial degrees are bounded uniformly,
and the union of the spectra over all positive widths is finite.

This answers the existence part of the uniform-upper-degree question in
Krzysztof Wojt's PR5168. It gives neither the least degree nor the coefficients
of a practical annihilator. PR5168's exact one-slot degree 881 and Ali
Romman's PR5382 lower bound 1093 at widths at least two are cited results.
They are compatible with this upper bound and are not recomputed here.

## A finite enclosing group

Work first with arbitrary n>=1. Let S be a symmetric n-by-n matrix over
F_2, let d(S) be its diagonal as a column, and let h(c) be any squarefree
Boolean polynomial of degree at most two with zero constant term. Define

    T(S,h)|c,a> = (-1)^[(d(S)+Sc).a+h(c)] |c,a+Sc>.

There are m=n(n+1)/2 independent entries of S. The polynomials h have
n linear and n(n-1)/2 quadratic coefficients, also m in total. The normal
forms therefore have 2^(2m)=2^[n(n+1)] parameter choices.

These choices give distinct maps. The permutation recovers Sc for every c,
hence recovers S. With S fixed, evaluate the sign at a=0 to recover h(c).
Squarefree Boolean monomials are independent as functions: their linear
coefficients are recovered at e_i and their quadratic coefficients at
e_i+e_j after subtracting the two linear values. Thus the group below has
exactly this many elements. This is the cardinality of an enclosing group,
not a claim about the order of the subgroup generated by the actual ports.

Put z_S(c)=d(S)+Sc. Applying the right map first gives

    T(S,h) T(U,k) = T(S+U, h+k+z_S(c).Uc).                 (1)

Indeed the right map contributes z_U(c).a+k(c). The left map contributes
z_S(c).(a+Uc)+h(c). The terms multiplying a combine to z_(S+U)(c).a.
The translation is a+(S+U)c. The remaining cross term has degree at most
two and has zero constant term. Reducing c_i^2 to c_i preserves that form.

The identity is T(0,0). The inverse, on either side, is

    T(S,h)^-1 = T(S, h+z_S(c).Sc).                        (2)

This also follows directly by applying the map twice. Associativity follows
from composition of the actual signed permutations, and injectivity makes
(1) a group law on the parameters. Call this finite group G_n.

For each actual port q, take S=q q^T and h=0. Since d(S)=q and
Sc=(q.c)q, its normal form is exactly Gamma_q. Thus all actual generators
belong to G_9. The enclosing order is

    |G_9| = 2^[9*10] = 2^90.                             (3)

No quotient by scalar signs is taken. In particular h is essential.
For example with n=2, S=diag(1,0), and U exchanging the two coordinates,
the cross term is c_2+c_1 c_2. Discarding it changes the operator. Some
enclosing maps have order four even though each actual port is an involution.

## Matrix group algebra and a common polynomial

Let Q[G_9] be the rational group algebra, with its actual group basis [g].
Form the single element

    B = sum_q r_q r_q^T [Gamma_q] in Mat_8(Q[G_9]).        (4)

All coefficients are integers. Let L be its left regular action on
Q^8 tensor Q[G_9]. This is an integer matrix of dimension

    D = 8 |G_9| = 2^93.

The matrix-algebra regular action is faithful. If an element acts as zero,
apply it to every e_j tensor [identity]. These vectors recover every entry
of every coefficient matrix, so the element itself is zero.

Let p(t)=det(tI-L). Cayley-Hamilton gives p(L)=0. Faithfulness therefore
gives p(B)=0 inside Mat_8(Q[G_9]). This is a monic integer polynomial of
degree D. Its definition is finite and exact; no coefficients of this
enormous determinant are claimed to have been generated.

For each f>=1 the assignment

    [g] -> g^tensor(f)

extends linearly to a unital algebra homomorphism. This uses
(gh)^tensor(f)=g^tensor(f) h^tensor(f). It does not send a sum to the
tensor power of that sum. Extend the map entrywise to the matrix algebra.
Equation (4) then maps to 4A_f. Thus

    p(4A_f)=0 for every f>=1.

Set P(x)=4^-D p(4x). It is monic and rational, is independent of f, and
has degree D. This proves the theorem. The same similarity argument
transfers the identity out of Bell coordinates.

Every eigenvalue of every A_f is a root of P, so there are at most D
distinct eigenvalues in their union. The common fixed vector |0,0>
embeds A_f as an invariant restriction of A_(f+1). Therefore their spectra
are nested, and eventually stabilize as sets. This argument supplies no
useful stabilization width or exact stabilized spectrum.

## Scope and verification

This is a scoped algebraic existence proof. It is not a finite certificate
of the huge characteristic polynomial, a new lower bound, a search failure,
or a complete multiplication theorem. The 2^93 degree bound is deliberately
coarse. It does not identify the actual generated subgroup, exploit the
particular root coefficients, price a scalar-polynomial decoder, or provide
an executable native implementation. All physical access, coefficient,
precision, ownership, restoration, tape, compiler and supplier costs remain.

The new verifier checks full normal-form injectivity at n=1,2,3 and every
ordered enclosing-group product at n=1,2 by literal signed permutations.
It checks the new cross term for all ordered actual-port generator pairs
using Boolean interpolation and a separate monomial expansion. A separate
small two-by-two matrix group-algebra example computes its regular
characteristic polynomial, checks determinant values by rational elimination,
and evaluates the same polynomial in three tensor representations. These
finite calculations corroborate the written proof; they do not enumerate G_9.

Classical ingredients are Boolean multilinear normal forms, the group
algebra regular representation and Cayley-Hamilton. David Ganz's PR4885
uses finite group-algebra annihilation for a different resonant channel.
The contribution here is the explicit quadratic signed-translation envelope
for this Bell action and its width-independent Gram consequence. No global
literature priority is claimed. Independent human and formal proof-kernel
review are unclaimed.
